# SVG Tracer

## Use

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. Use the sidebar to browse for a PNG, JPEG, WebP, BMP, or GIF, or expand **Try an example**. Adjust the options, choose **Trace SVG**, then **Save SVG**. The main workspace compares the source and result; expandable panels contain the SVG source and detailed trace statistics. On a narrow screen, the header menu opens the sidebar; Escape closes it.

All image processing stays in the browser. The tool uses the local shared `../one.css` and `../one.js`, the adjacent DOM-free `tracer.js`, and local fonts. There are no external libraries, network requests, uploads, or build steps. Keep those files together when copying the tool. Tracing runs in a local Blob Worker; **Cancel** terminates it. Browser restrictions that block workers are reported without discarding the source image.

Start with the defaults. Keep **Protect thin outlines and small accents** enabled for outlined artwork. For a known palette, enter the paint count, including an opaque background but excluding transparency. **Colors** accepts 0 for Auto or an explicit count up to 64; Auto may discover up to 32 paints. Set **Minimum region area** to 0 to disable area-based merging; color inference still determines component ownership. Increasing boundary tolerance permits simpler geometry. Noise cleanup at 0 disables the prefilter and source-based boundary-fragment correction while retaining antialias decoding.

The working dimension limits computation while preserving the original dimensions in the exported SVG. Downsampling can erase a narrow feature before tracing begins. Changing options clears stale results and requires another trace. Invalid or unreadable imports retain the previous source and result. GIF input traces a decoded frame rather than exporting animation.

## Algorithm

Palette discovery favors flat interior samples rather than edge shadows. A separate directional evidence pass finds coherent narrow paints and compact accents without promoting every compression fringe to a paint. Small paint cores take priority over longer coreless fringes; near-black and near-white candidates are normalized before checking whether the paint already exists. Coreless candidates need stronger evidence against existing paint mixtures as noise cleanup increases. Antialias decoding uses spatially supported paints and two- or three-paint mixtures. A gray pixel between black and white is not assigned a remote blue paint merely because blue is nearer in a perceptual color space.

After noise cleanup, newly recovered Auto paints also need distinct color evidence in the original input samples at their support sites. A filtered plateau whose source centroid matches a base paint is marked as an alias. All discovered paints still participate in pixel assignment and mixture decoding: deleting a shade earlier can move otherwise unrelated pixels by changing their available paint evidence. After decoding, aliases merge into their supported base paints; mixture weights and boundary evidence are updated consistently. The check uses the original RGBA input directly and does not require every noisy neighbor of a tiny accent to be flat. Explicit counts and noise-zero tracing do not use this additional merging.

After area cleanup, original working-raster RGB can correct a disconnected fragment that incorrectly uses a paint present elsewhere. Only opaque fragments smaller than another component of the same paint qualify. A pixel must touch exactly two other paints in its eight-neighbor neighborhood; other junction pixels remain unresolved. The original RGB must fit that two-paint mixture substantially better than every mixture where the fragment's own paint has the largest of three coverage weights, including weights below one half. Error allowances use separate 95th-percentile source deviations from interior samples of the relevant paints, excluding the candidate component from its own paint's reference. This avoids treating a large background as evidence about a rare accent. The tails are empirical noise estimates, not guarantees for arbitrary boundary or correlated noise. This correction applies to Auto and explicit counts with positive noise cleanup, independently of the minimum-area setting. Stale mixture evidence is discarded before rebuilding the boundary graph; the palette itself is retained.

An explicit paint count refines the strongest varying RGB histogram buckets, preserving distinct flat paints that share a coarse bucket. Detail recovery protects those separated paints from coarse averages and antialias fringes. Refinement is bounded by the requested count, so strongly varying noisy regions can still outrank a weak similar-paint distinction; Auto retains its conservative noise handling.

Automatic detail recovery requires support inside the opaque footprint, in an isolated mark, or in the RGB of partially transparent edge pixels. A repeated opaque fringe along a transparent silhouette alone is insufficient evidence for another paint. This also prevents false edge paints from fragmenting otherwise round or straight boundaries. RGB below the topology alpha cutoff is retained for this evidence pass, while very low alpha is excluded because unpremultiplication makes its RGB unstable.

One planar boundary graph records region topology. Neighboring regions reuse the same fitted geometry in opposite directions, including holes. Boundary samples use a local square-pixel coverage model; binary boundaries retain lattice evidence, and transparent edges use source alpha. Complete circles, ellipses, and polygons are tried first. Other candidates include lines, circular and elliptical arcs, cubic Bezier curves, rounded rectangles, and capsules. With detail protection enabled, long, strongly supported straight sections on thin boundaries are fitted separately from adjoining bends so a broad cubic cannot bow an otherwise straight edge. Their adjoining curves use the line's tangent; smooth curve subdivisions share tangent directions, while detected sharp corners remain corners. Multiple boundary chains can share polygon support lines or circle and ellipse guides across color junctions.

Long straight sections of binary contours use support lines fitted to all lattice samples. A diagonal's alternating pixel corners can otherwise force small curves that reproduce a staircase despite a clear straight edge. Candidate runs must span at least 32 working pixels, with RMS support-line residual at most 0.45 pixels and maximum residual at most 0.85 pixels; the requested fitting tolerance still bounds the accepted geometry. Shared chain endpoints stay fixed, and adjoining curves meet the accepted line with its tangent. Shorter runs use the existing fitters.

Projecting a straight support can move its neighboring span's endpoints off the lattice. The remaining interior samples retain their binary interpretation, preventing a round cap's short pixel plateau from becoming a false straight edge. Sharp corners close to a paint junction can also survive fitting; their shorter available support requires a stronger, consistent turn at both measurement scales. Shallow turns near junctions remain conservative to avoid adding corners to smooth curves.

Binary bends between nearly parallel straight supports can also fit a local circular terminal. The circle is constrained to meet both support tangents; its tangency points can adjust the support endpoints, while shared chain endpoints stay fixed. The candidate must stay within the requested tolerance and one working pixel in both distance directions, with RMS error at most 0.45 pixels. This bounded model reduces some residual cap waviness without requiring the whole shape to be a capsule. Unsupported caps retain their ordinary curve fit.

Contained capsule contours also fit a rotated centerline and radius jointly, giving them straight sides and tangent circular ends. Acceptance checks both directions of contour error, with a maximum of 0.85 working pixels and RMS of 0.45, further restricted by the requested tolerance. A capsule must improve RMS error over an available polygon fit by at least 5% before replacing it. Circle and ellipse recognition takes priority, and comparable axis-aligned rounded rectangles retain their native SVG representation. This model applies to free loops, not chains interrupted by junctions or the image frame.

Polygon guides must respect the image frame. A boundary between two regions inside the image cannot run along the frame, where one side necessarily belongs to the outside face. Near a clipped corner, projecting a junction onto a polygon can otherwise add a backtracking border segment. Such proposals are rejected before they move junctions or trigger tighter fits that would reproduce binary pixel stairs.

Native circles and ellipses are emitted when the neighboring face is unpainted. When both sides are painted, matching arc commands avoid differences between primitive and path rendering. Thin faces receive tighter fitting tolerances when detail protection is enabled: opposite boundaries can each consume an error allowance, so fitting them too loosely can visibly vary an outline's width even when it remains connected. The automatic cap retains a quarter-pixel minimum on the narrowest sampled features to avoid chasing antialias noise; an explicitly lower boundary tolerance still takes priority. This protection can require more segments around curves. Spatially distributed ownership witnesses protect local features; speckle cleanup exempts coherent elongated marks when detail protection is enabled.

A fitting span with only two samples remains a straight segment. Estimated tangents must not bend such an unsupported span into a subpixel loop, especially where several paints meet. This preserves local junction topology without forcing an otherwise smooth perimeter onto the pixel grid.

A thick miter or merged intersection can make an otherwise narrow connected outline appear globally thick. For these networks, local inward depth samples on each side of a boundary restore thin-feature fitting limits. An area-to-perimeter check excludes broad filled regions whose corner samples can look misleadingly thin. Regions already recognized as thin retain their established tolerance. More faithful boundaries can require substantially more SVG segments.

Crossings, junction order, holes, region ownership, and assembled-chain fidelity are checked. When the chord approximation leaves a close approach uncertain, affected curved chains are flattened once at finer precision before changing their fits. The separation margin uses the sum of their flattening error bounds, and every topology check runs again. Unresolved interfaces are retried locally while independent successful fits remain. Each retry halves the actual fitting tolerance, including any thin-detail cap, so retries cannot repeat the same capped fit. An exact pixel-boundary fallback handles difficult topology. Statistics report recovered paints, mixture decoding, feature checks, repairs, and fallbacks. Numerical validation is not a formal proof for arbitrary curves.

Fitted contours also preserve sampled clearance between nearby parts of the same boundary that are far apart along the curve. If fitting consumes more than 0.75 working pixels of that separation, the chain retries at tighter tolerance, dropping an incompatible guide if necessary. Each retry keeps the initial local search extent so tightening cannot hide the pair that rejected the fit. This protects some narrow spiral openings even when both edges satisfy their individual distance tolerances. It can add segments; sparse samples, unresolved subpixel gaps and tapered tips still limit reconstruction.

## Limits

The tool traces visible, non-overlapping flat-color regions. Hidden geometry, original object identities, stroke centerlines, and original layer order are not recovered. Outlines remain actual filled regions. No embedded raster images, masks, underpainting, overlap expansion, or seam-covering strokes are added. Separate fills may still show screen-space antialias seams in some SVG renderers.

Auto paint counts, corner recognition, primitive recognition, and background removal are heuristic. Broad shadows can resemble intentional fills; small or similar paints may require an explicit color count. Background removal cannot semantically distinguish a background from another dominant border-connected paint. Cleanup can delete intentional small details even with detail protection.

An intentional narrow color strip touching transparency can be indistinguishable from an opaque compositing fringe. Auto can omit such edge-only accents; set the known paint count to retain them. Conversely, a fringe whose RGB remains consistent through an alpha ramp can resemble a real outline and still be recovered. Transparency is separate from the reported paint count: blue, orange, and transparency require two paints.

Gradients, partial-opacity vector paints, font recovery, and subpixel details without surviving evidence are unsupported. Transparent input uses a 50% alpha cutoff for topology, then alpha coverage to estimate the contour. Coverage inversion assumes approximately sRGB, square-pixel antialiasing; other filters and gamma conventions can change the fit. JPEG variation is treated as generic noise; there is no watermark detector. Safety limits are 4 million working pixels, 5,000 regions, and 450,000 boundary edges.

## Development and verification

`tracer.js` contains the self-contained `createTracer()` factory with no DOM dependency. The browser adapter in `index.html` runs the same core in a worker and exposes `window.FlatLogoTracer.trace(image, options, progress)` for synchronous integrations. Image input is `{width, height, data}` with RGBA bytes; options and tolerances use working pixels. `outputWidth` and `outputHeight` set the exported dimensions.

Run the bundled image and invalid-input regression checks from this directory:

```sh
bun tests/test.cjs
```

The runner also works with Node. It uses bundled RGBA fixtures and checks paint counts, unchanged input bytes, finite SVG output, numerical topology validation, and independently closed and connected boundary rings. Cases cover primitives, overlapping objects, thin outlines, small accents, transparency, concave polygons, rounded shapes, organic curves, holes, narrow bridges, JPEG noise, remote-paint antialias decoding, large palettes, four-way contacts, tiny pixels, and seeded pixel-art fields. The original [edge_artifacts fixture](tests/fixtures/edge_artifacts.png) adds opaque RGB fringes and a short false-color strip to clean geometric artwork; it checks the recovered circle's center and radius and the diagonal polygon's straight sides and vertices. Separate transparent outline and isolated accent fixtures protect real thin paints. Regenerate these three PNG/RGBA pairs with `bun tests/artwork.js`. No copyrighted reference artwork is used in the tests. Fixture checks establish behavior on those inputs, not universal reconstruction accuracy.

Run the focused contour and similar-paint regressions from the repository root:

```sh
bun test svg_tracer/tests/capsule-contours.test.js svg_tracer/tests/paint-quality.test.js
```

The paint tests preserve known colors and separate regions with and without noise cleanup or detail protection, including antialiased boundaries, fringe interference, excessive requested counts, and 64 paints in one coarse bucket. Auto noise controls check that finer explicit sampling does not promote noise by default.

Run the browser regressions from the repository root with Bun and Playwright installed. Keep the actual shared `one.css` and `one.js` beside the `svg_tracer` directory: the UI checks exercise their visibility, feedback, and mobile drawer behavior directly.

```sh
bun test svg_tracer/tests/browser.test.js svg_tracer/tests/quality.browser.test.js svg_tracer/tests/outlined.browser.test.js
```

The supplied `tests/parrot.jpg` is included with permission as a permanent regression fixture. Its browser checks protect source-supported paint assignment at noise 10 and 20.

The Chromium checks open the tool directly from `file://` and exercise real worker tracing, both examples, downloads, original export dimensions, matching preview sizes, stale-result invalidation, invalid imports, cancellation, blocked workers, and the image-decoding fallback. Layout checks include expanded output panels, long filenames, 320-1440 pixel viewports, automatic light and dark modes, and keyboard operation of the mobile drawer. They also check for page errors and external network requests. Other browser engines are not covered by this suite.

The quality checks rasterize exported SVG in Chromium and compare mean absolute premultiplied RGBA error on a 0-255 scale. Background removal is disabled for this comparison; its semantics are checked separately in the core suite. The damaged geometric fixture is compared against the generator's clean source. `tests/raster-comparison.json` records the fixed fixtures' reference errors and enforced ceilings, with small rounding allowances. These finite comparisons establish corpus preservation rather than a guarantee for arbitrary artwork.

Curve-node reduction already runs automatically inside each smooth fitting span: the coalescing loop in `fitSmoothSpan` repeatedly tries replacing adjacent fitted segments with one cubic while preserving the outside tangents and staying within the geometric tolerance. It has no separate UI switch and does not compare rendered before-and-after image quality. Detected corners, straight supports, shared chain endpoints, and independently fitted spans limit which joins it can remove.

The sampler check measures both full-image error and outline paint coverage across 90 normals on the red shape's straight sides and bottom, solving the white/red/dark RGB mixture before summing coverage. It enforces mean outline-width error below 0.13 working pixels and full-image RGBA error below 0.57. Additional normal profiles cover the visible circle, all four rounded rectangle corners, the ellipse's underside, and the red top edge. These compare each outline's width and center with the source pixels, preserving the generator's different stroke widths. Rendering the SVG at eight times the source dimensions and area-averaging before integration reduces screen-space antialias seams so they do not dominate geometric width measurements. Native-resolution errors are checked separately around occlusion junctions. A straight top edge must also retain its source centerline without bowing; matching widths alone would miss both boundaries drifting together. These are sampled checks, not proof that every curve or junction is exact, and native renderer seams remain possible.

Fixed PNG fixtures also exercise rasterizer-dependent staircase failures: `tests/fixtures/firefox-sampler.png` contains native Firefox 155.0 pixels from Linux x86_64, with sampler shapes translated by (0.7, 0.7) working pixels after painting the white background; `tests/fixtures/chromium-sampler.png` uses Chromium with translation (0.8, 0). The Firefox case checks underdetermined junction fits, and the Chromium case checks close-approach validation precision. Both trace with default options and require curved exported outline subpaths without an exact-pixel fallback or long staircase runs. Keep these captured pixels rather than regenerating them in the current test browser: canvas antialiasing varies across engines and platforms. These fixtures reproduce the failure class, not the unavailable source pixels from every platform.

Independent procedural capsules check clipped binary-alpha edges at two slopes and four quarter turns. Pixel-center distance tests generate the artwork without reference images or browser antialiasing. Default traces must retain a compact curved contour and bounded raster error. Geometry complexity matters here: following every binary pixel step can score better on raster error while producing a visibly stair-stepped vector edge.

Fully contained procedural capsules add free-loop checks at two more slopes and four rotations. They bound side deviation, serialized raster error, and geometry complexity, and require curved ends without exact fallback or frame contact. The focused contour tests measure normal intersections along the sides and tangent changes around the caps, catching bowed sides and polygonal ends that compactness or low pixel error alone can miss. Independent holdouts vary radius, length, angle, subpixel placement, and binary versus 4-by-4 square-pixel coverage. Chamfered polygons, ellipses, and rounded rectangles serve as negative controls against overrecognition. These checks establish sampled contour recovery for contained shapes; shared junctions and other rasterizers require separate coverage. The copyrighted supplied-image fixtures and derived comparison are not distributed.

`tests/outlined.browser.test.js` adds 96 seeded compositions and 64 focused contact cases. The compositions combine irregular concave stars, organic curves, holes with connecting interior strokes, and overlapping bent ribbons, with round or miter joins, subpixel placement, black or dark outlines 1.5-6 working pixels wide, and rotations throughout a full turn. Each image is encoded and decoded as PNG or high-quality JPEG (quality 0.94) before tracing with default Auto settings; 16 cases use transparent PNG. Focused cases rotate acute, obtuse, shared, and near-tangent outline contacts through 16 angles. The two short one-pixel mark controls ensure compression-fringe rejection does not simply discard every paint without a thick core.

These tests separately limit full-image error, error on source edge neighborhoods, and error within six working pixels of source stroke intersections. White and dark backdrop comparisons also check the displayed result, including antialias transparency seams. Junction locations come from the generator's independent stroke masks, not traced geometry. An independently drawn pre-codec visible-stroke mask checks total outline weight; RGB paint-mixture fitting estimates the output's dark coverage. Worst-junction checks separately bound local outline-weight error and lost dark pixels so an intact region cannot compensate for a damaged join elsewhere. A local proximity check catches erased dark pixels, and source-white probes between near-tangent shapes require a bright separator even on a black backdrop; a partly transparent antialias seam alone is not a closed gap. Aggregate ceilings prevent large flat areas or a few easy cases from hiding widespread edge degradation. The outline-weight estimate is approximate at multi-paint junctions, and the gap probes are sampled checks rather than a formal topology proof. JPEG pixel errors target the decoded input, while its stroke-weight reference targets the clean pre-codec artwork.
