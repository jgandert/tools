# Contour and noise experiments

These bounded studies explain the source-paint alias, contour-fitting, and clearance decisions and identify unresolved tracing failures. Core hashes and measurements refer to the implementations evaluated in each study. They do not establish the behavior or performance of every later checkout; use the retained runners and regression tests to evaluate a changed core. Disposable reports under `.quality-work/` are not distributed.

## Binary round terminals

A fitted straight support can move the endpoints of its neighboring span away from the pixel lattice. Checking every point to decide whether that span contains binary evidence then classifies the span incorrectly. The antialiased straight-run detector can accept a short vertical pixel plateau on a round cap as a true straight edge. Classification now uses the untouched interior samples, preserving the binary support-line criteria after endpoint projection.

The independent bent-ribbon test rasterizes the union of two thick segments by pixel-center distance, with radii 18, 30, and 42, three oblique angles, and two subpixel placements. Its terminal is mathematically circular, while the bend prevents whole-shape capsule recognition. Across the 18 configurations, the baseline produced six flat terminal spans of 15-17 pixels. The corrected contours have no terminal chord longer than 2.98 pixels in the validated flattened geometry; worst radial error remains below 0.92 pixels. Total segment count decreased from 318 to 277. The test bounds radial error, long flat spans, paint/region preservation, and exact fallbacks. Existing contained and clipped capsule tests provide separate straight-side, chamfer, ellipse, and rounded-rectangle controls.

A separate tangent-radius experiment increased the quadratic tangent estimation radius from 8 to 12 pixels. Mean radial error decreased from 0.351 to 0.312 pixels on the same 18 shapes, but the number of cases containing an almost flat cap window increased from two to five. That probe samples radial intersections at 0.025-radian intervals and measures the middle point's perpendicular distance from a chord extending about five pixels in each direction; its near-flat threshold is 0.05 pixels. It is a local bend diagnostic, not a curvature proof. The mixed result does not support a general radius increase; it is not integrated.

A local terminal model further reduces waviness when a binary span has two nearly parallel straight supports. It solves for a circle tangent to both supports within their one-parameter family, using geometric least squares on samples away from the straight sides. Support tangency points may move by up to 16 working pixels; shared chain endpoints stay fixed. A candidate must have radius at least four pixels and a sweep within 0.15 radians of a semicircle, then pass maximum bidirectional error `min(tolerance, 1)` and RMS error 0.45 pixels. The assembled chain still passes the existing fidelity, clearance and topology checks. Antialiased spans and spans without sufficient straight support retain their normal fitting path.

The 18 bent-ribbon configurations improve terminal radial error in ten cases and leave eight unchanged, with no measured radial regression or new fallback. The ray probe samples angles -1.4 through 1.4 at 0.025-radian intervals relative to the outward terminal axis, taking the nearest positive intersection with the flattened contour. Mean absolute radial error across those samples decreases from 0.348 to 0.201 working pixels; worst radial error remains 0.879 in an unsupported case. Each bend window uses `round(5 / (radius * 0.025))` samples on either side and measures the middle sample's perpendicular distance to the outer chord. Windows below 0.05 pixels decrease from four to one. This is a finite local-bend measurement, not a curvature proof. The radius30, angle-0.75, shift0.4 case retains the remaining window and a 0.879-pixel radial error.

`bent-contours.test.js` now requires three supported radii to stay within 0.3 pixels radially and retain at least 80% of their source circle's chord bend. A separate Chromium check samples the serialized SVG path with `getPointAtLength()` every 0.2 pixels, retaining cap points at least two pixels outward along the terminal axis and within radius+4 of its center. The three new supported controls stay within 0.046, 0.195 and 0.242 pixels radially. Model-derived tangency coordinates retain full precision because rounding a nearly semicircular chord can move the center reconstructed by the SVG renderer. It also rasterizes 24 independently noncircular ends: ellipse and chamfer terminals at radii8/18/30, angles-0.75/-0.45 and shifts0/0.4. Ellipse ends use an axial radius 1.3 times their half-width. Chamfers use `u >= -radius`, `abs(v) <= radius` and
`-u + abs(v) <= 1.45 * radius` in the terminal frame; their visible corners must retain a sampled turn above 0.3 radians. All 24 retain identical terminal radial and bend measurements before and after the local-circle change. Some full SVGs change at the independently circular opposite tail, so exact SVG equality is not the negative-control contract. Independent review checked the tangent-family equations, call path, before/after measurements and negative controls before disposable reports were removed. These controls support the local binary model; they do not establish cap recovery for arbitrary widths, sampling, clipping or junctions.

## Tapered terminals and narrow openings

`bun tests/campaign-contour-study.js --out .quality-work/tip-limits.json` reproduces the five authored Archimedean ribbons, including the diagnostic-only `spiral-gap1.3-limit`. Choose an unused output path. `spiral-diagnostics.js` shares the original 640-step polygon generator with the runner and independently intersects its closed source path with transects. `bun test tests/spiral-diagnostics.test.js` checks holes, vertex contacts, reversed rays, component identity, missing local strips, isolated fragments and analytic source widths. The study validates finite, positive source intervals and well-formed measured intervals even for its diagnostic-only case; this measurement check supplies no recovery-quality pass bound.

Nearest-boundary queries inspect every chain separating the two paints. A chain with the most flattened vertices need not contain the terminal or queried opening, especially when the paint or background is fragmented. `nearestStartBoundary` and `nearestEndBoundary` report raw-lattice, sampled and fitted distances separately. They are proximity measurements, not correspondence or proof of recovered tips. Local source-distance windows also include all relevant chains rather than silently becoming empty when another chain contains the requested location.

Tip transects are perpendicular to the authored centerline at arclength offsets 0.5/1/2/4/8/12 pixels from each terminal, using interpolated lengths of the 640 centerline chords. Their intersections measure cross-section occupancy and width, not perpendicular displacement from the sloping taper edges. The independent source interval contains the centerline. Separate longitudinal rays follow the centerline's endpoint tangent into the ribbon; these straight rays differ from the curving centerline. Gap transects use outward normals at theta `pi + [-0.2,-0.1,0,0.1,0.2]`. Width is constant there, so these are also normals to the authored boundary. The reported source opening is the interval between consecutive source crossings, not pitch minus twice half-width or the distance between unrelated parameter points on the two turns.

For each transect, all face loops of the requested paint supply raw, sampled and fitted intervals, retaining their component IDs. Only intervals with positive overlap with the independent source interval are matched; absent strips remain empty arrays, and multiple fragments remain separate. A remote arm cannot stand in for a missing strip. Positive overlap establishes local correspondence, not full endpoint or width fidelity, and sparse transects do not prove connectivity. Partial source pixels around each terminal and the source pixel containing each normal station retain decoded two-paint RGB coverage, labels and component IDs. Four-neighbor component counts compare final pre-fit labels with the source coverage thresholded at 50%. These fixtures use an opaque, known two-paint canvas mixture and `minArea:0`; the label comparison does not characterize arbitrary palettes, codecs, cleanup or alpha edges.

For `spiral-gap1.3-limit` (pitch 22.9, half-width 10.8, shift (0.19,-0.24), noise 0, tolerance 0.15), all labels agree exactly with 50% source coverage. Its 20 background and 3 foreground components therefore already exist before sampling or fitting. The theta-pi source-normal opening is 1.128 pixels, versus raw/sample/fitted widths 1.271/1.061/1.285; the local background interval belongs to an enclosed component, not an uninterrupted open spiral passage. The five sampled background transects belong to components 13/15/15/18/20. Finite local widths do not establish that the passage is connected.

The start's nearest raw/sample/fitted distances are 0.049/0.335/0.335 pixels; the end's are 1.296/1.818/1.797. At end offsets 0.5/1, the source cross-section widths are 0.157/0.313 pixels and raw, sampled and fitted intervals are all absent. Their containing source pixels still have foreground coverage 0.146/0.247, below majority ownership. This is loss at ownership/contour extraction, not absence of source evidence or a proof that recovery is impossible. A tiny foreground island crosses the raw longitudinal ray at 1.546 pixels, but the sampled/fitted ray first meets another island at 2.946; the main foreground component begins at 4.346 raw and 4.100 sampled/fitted pixels. Neither the nearest-boundary distance nor the main-component onset alone describes this fragmented terminal. Sampling can further truncate a local ray before curve fitting starts.

No production geometry or segmentation change follows from this diagnosis. Tighter fitting cannot connect components already separated by ownership or restore a missing pre-fit interval. Resume recovery with a bounded model that uses surviving subthreshold coverage to infer tapered terminals or multiple crossings per pixel, then checks the recovered local occupancy and passage connectivity against independent source geometry. It must distinguish continuous tapers from intentionally disconnected small paints and preserve rotated/subpixel, transparent, noisy, thin-outline and existing topology controls. Changing a global ownership cutoff or fitting an assumed taper from proximity alone lacks that evidence.

## Corners near paint junctions

An open boundary can contain a sharp corner only a few pixels from its endpoint at a paint junction. Excluding an entire tangent-support radius from corner detection forces that corner into a smooth cubic. In `a.jpg`, the highlight junction lies about three pixels above the lower corner; smoothing the nearby outer corner produces the small outward bulge shown in the supplied issue crop. The correction admits samples at least 1.5 contour pixels from the endpoint, requiring a turn of at least 1.2 radians and at least 80% of the larger-scale turn when support is truncated. This intentionally remains conservative for shallower near-junction corners.

The independent browser controls draw a three-paint polygon with a curved highlight ending near its corner. At a 55-degree corner with highlight gaps of five and eight pixels, distance from the mathematical corner to the traced contour decreases from 2.093 and 1.841 pixels to about 1.03 pixels. A 75-degree, three-pixel-gap case also improves. A longer-gap case and two smooth ellipse contacts protect unaffected behavior. The permanent tests bound corner distance, local contour deviation, paint interfaces, and exact fallbacks. A separate 55-degree, three-pixel-gap attempt did not reproduce the supplied image's defect; the synthetic tests establish the nearby-corner failure class, not an exact recreation of that photograph. One exploratory 35-degree case improved corner distance while increasing maximum nearby deviation from 0.73 to 1.13 pixels, showing why both measurements matter.

The unrestricted near-endpoint proposal added extra corners and exact fallbacks on heavily compressed artwork. Requiring the stronger turn limits reduces those effects. The final contour changes leave the clean generated sheet's default trace unchanged. They can still increase segment counts on noisy inputs, and small image-wide error increases remain possible even when the known corner or cap improves. Residual subpixel cap waviness and shallow-corner errors are not claimed solved.

## Noise and compression

The study varies noise over 0, 10, 20, and 40, and recompresses the generated sheet at JPEG qualities 0.95, 0.8, and 0.6. Stronger cleanup is not a monotonic quality control: the local range-gated average can create broader edge plateaus, and detail recovery can mistake them for another paint. Conversely, increasing palette-merging distances indiscriminately can erase genuine similar-color accents.

The campaign baseline at the default 1024-pixel working limit gives the original sheet nine paints and 20 regions with noise 10, but ten paints and 236 regions with noise 40. At its native 1254-pixel size, noise 40 gives nine paints and 19 regions. Reproduction must therefore preserve the working-size reduction. At JPEG quality 0.95, baseline noise 20 or 40 gives nine paints and 20 regions; at quality 0.6, baseline noise settings all leave extra paints and many regions. Image-wide error against the original sheet remains approximately 1.20-1.56 across these settings, which conceals large differences in vector complexity. Source verification described below removes many of these extra paints.

The supplied feet reference exhibits subtle navy/teal islands at default noise. Comparing the baseline SVG against the manually cleaned SVG at 544 by 534 pixels, a navy reference mask eroded by two four-neighbor passes contains 69,628 pixels. Of these, 964 differ from reference navy by more than eight RGB Euclidean units and 534 by more than sixteen; none differ by more than 24. This does not establish the exact original editing settings or semantic correctness of every reference change. Noise 20 gives 25 regions versus 155 at noise 10 and is a useful input-specific workaround. The supplied swirl at noise 40 retains a false mint-colored paint near its narrow cream openings.

Three denoising proposals were withheld. Removing the 16-channel cap on `nearPaint` suppresses the swirl's false paint, but also removes the real `a.jpg` highlight at noise 40. Requiring wider color support only for candidates explained by an existing paint mixture rejects a false four-by-four boundary patch, but also rejects an intentional isolated four-by-four accent with exactly the same mixture color in Auto. Explicit colors retain that accent. Finally, checking proximity to both contributing paints preserves the isolated accent while rejecting the boundary patch, but the supplied swirl gains regions (51 to 66), and the feet image merely replaces one spurious shade with another. The straightforward proximity prototype also stores core-pixel indices across the image and added roughly 12-17% to single-run quantization times on the large diagnostic images; those unreplicated timings are not a stable performance estimate. None of these three proposals is integrated.

The compact color ambiguity can be reproduced without supplied artwork: make an opaque 64-by-64 bitmap with the left half `[20,50,90]` and right half `[210,150,40]`. Insert a four-by-four patch of their midpoint `[115,100,65]`, first centered at `(32,32)` on the boundary and then at `(16,32)` inside the left half. Compare Auto with explicit `colors: 3`, and include a six-by-six midpoint patch, an eight-by-eight isolated midpoint accent, a one-pixel off-mixture strip `[115,120,105]` at column 32, and a four-by-four accent `[240,225,80]` centered at `(16,32)`. Use noise 10. The broader-support proposal samples the eight horizontal, vertical, and diagonal neighbors two pixels away, then requires four such core samples when squared RGB residual to a palette-pair mixture is below
`3 * max(7, noise)^2`. The proximity proposal adds a three-pixel neighborhood check for both explaining paints only when wider support is absent. These controls expose the ambiguity; they are not a reliable noise classifier.

### Shared boundary source prediction

The [shared predictor](shared-boundary-model.js) is a benchmark-only attempt to explain a suspect patch from surrounding source samples, rather than fitting a separate flexible RGB mixture at each pixel. It does not run inside `createTracer()` and supplies no production option. It evaluates frozen baseline components of at most 128 working pixels with exactly two adjacent paints A/B; size limits the diagnostic scope rather than establishing that a component is noise. The baseline palette, component mask and proposal orientation still derive from the complete input. Only subsequent source estimation withholds the target RGB, so the prediction is held out conditional on that frozen discovery, not a completely blind end-to-end inference.

The component's principal axis defines tangential and normal coordinates. The full target interval and a two-pixel guard are omitted from raw-RGB plateau estimation, source RGB-midpoint crossings and color-profile fitting. Source crossings fit one quadratic boundary; a separate cubic variant tests whether an additional shared geometry coefficient addresses underfit. Both use the same source-geometry RMS/max limits of 0.45/0.85 working pixels, rank checks and two-sided interpolation requirement. A single piecewise-linear RGB profile uses fixed normal-distance knots `[-4,-2,-.5,0,.5,2,4]`, learned plateau endpoints and 15 interior coefficients shared across every training section. RGB predictions are clipped to byte range. A consistent source-color midpoint offset can be absorbed into the profile; it is not proof of the original vector boundary's location.

Validation withholds two other complete intervals of the target's length, one on each side, eight pixels away before their guards. Thus four working pixels remain between guarded intervals. Each fit must bracket every combined excluded gap with source observations on both ends and cover the required normal distances. Held-out intervals refit geometry, endpoints and the shared profile. Their mean, upper-tail, maximum and squared mean-bias errors form an empirical envelope; poor prediction causes abstention, with a mean-error ceiling `min(3*max(4,noise)^2, .04*||B-A||^2)`. Target ownership signs and RGB predictions must also agree between the main and validation fits. Target errors must fit the envelope and beat an antialiased C-dominant alternative by more than the held-out mean error. That alternative is the closest point in the RGB triangle with vertices C, `(C+A)/2` and
`(C+B)/2`, allowing at least 50% C coverage. These dependent, finite observations are not a statistical confidence bound or proof that C is absent.

Two training treatments are separate global variants. The conservative version excludes every other C component's guarded tangential interval. This can remove almost all training material when the baseline mistakes the same bleed for C repeatedly. The second allows off-target C observations and fits all channels with shared RGB Huber weights for four iterations, using cutoff `sqrt(3)*max(4,noise)` on the unclipped prediction residual. It still excludes the entire target and validation intervals. Genuine marks are not necessarily outliers under that cutoff, and a minority of all pixels can dominate a particular profile knot's observations. Exclusion and weighting change together, so differences cannot be attributed to robust weighting alone.

[The independent artwork generator](shared-boundary-artwork.js) uses a 160x160 source with boundary `x=80.25+(y-80)^2/400`, 16x16 square-pixel integration, a remote C swatch and an optional 0.75-pixel mark or three-paint junction. It separates luminance from RGB chroma, applies a fixed two-dimensional Gaussian chroma filter with sigma 1.2/radius four, then reconstructs rounded, clipped RGB with the original luminance. This generates source variation independently of the predictor's knots and regression. It is not a JPEG emulator; RGB clipping can change reconstructed luminance. Fractional pre-corruption paint coverage remains the source reference. The mark target includes 50% coverage, and source-known proposal tests conservatively retain genuine C at ties rather than using the generator's first-paint majority tie break.

A source-known diagnostic supplies correct surrounding labels and injects C only into eight target pixels. The quadratic predictor changes all eight to the known A paint in the blank bleed source, but retains all eight when the actual mark is present; both junction variants abstain. This establishes nonvacuous prediction ability, not automatic discovery. Using actual tracer proposals, the permissive-training quadratic variant removes 22 of 70 falsely C-colored boundary pixels at explicit3/noise10, leaving 48. Every changed pixel receives its known source paint. The accepted component's mean RGB error is 1.18, versus 15.37 for the C-dominant alternative and a worst held-out mean of 13.43; the empirical acceptance advantage is narrow. Its 633 profile samples all retain weight one, so the positive result does not demonstrate a benefit from Huber downweighting. The paired real-mark source preserves all eight target pixels and makes no changes; it still retains the other false boundary paint. Cubic source-known variants abstain rather than matching the quadratic positive.

No tested variant changes a parrot pixel. Crossing quadratic/cubic with conservative/permissive training at noise10/20 gives eight all-abstention runs. The main wing patch touches more than two paints, so it is a junction outside this model's scope. The default 17-pixel head patch fails the quadratic geometry bound: maximum off-patch residual 1.23 working pixels. Cubic geometry fits more closely, but its worst held-out mean RGB error is 1,255.51, or 1,309.32 with permissive robust training, against the 300 ceiling. Nearby sections therefore do not justify predicting away the head patch. Crest and flank proposals have competing crossings, insufficient interpolation or unavailable comparable validation. Loosening those checks would substitute an unsupported assumption for the prediction being tested.

All 128 seedless mark/blank model evaluations, crossing both geometries, clean/JPEG0.85, Auto/explicit3, noise10/20 and four variants, make no changes; the 16 correlated-column evaluations also make none. ### Source-calibrated boundary arms

The production `refineSourceOwnership` pass addresses a narrower wing-arm case that the shared-geometry predictor cannot classify. The original working-raster RGB along most of the brown strip already fits the ordinary body-red/blue segment closely; the decoder can nevertheless assign a remote paint when its local confident-seed search does not supply the appropriate pair. This pass works after decoding and area cleanup, without changing the palette, candidate discovery order or source colors. It uses the two paints actually touching each candidate pixel, not a larger confident-seed search or a flexible chroma rectangle.

Candidate components must be opaque, smaller than another component of the same paint and traced with positive noise cleanup. Eight-neighbor labels identify arms touching exactly two other paints; pixels with additional neighboring labels, including unresolved junction pixels, are left alone. Each arm shares its fixed paint pair. Source RGB is projected onto that ordinary segment, and both the arm's mean residual and every changed pixel's residual must fit the empirical allowance. The alternative retains the original paint C as the largest of three mixture weights, including ties: its RGB polygon has vertices C, `(C+A)/2`, `(C+A+B)/3` and `(C+B)/2`. A half-coverage-only C triangle is too restrictive for this decoder's plurality rule and is not used. Group proposals are evaluated against frozen labels and applied simultaneously.

Allowances come from squared original-RGB-to-nominal-paint deviations at pixels whose four cardinal neighbors share the same component. Maintain separate distributions for A, B and C; exclude the entire candidate component from C's reference and require at least 20 samples for every relevant paint. The two-paint fit ceiling is the larger A/B 95th percentile, with a three-unit squared-RGB rounding floor. Its advantage over the C-plurality model must exceed the largest A/B/C percentile or the same floor. Maximum-error calibration abstains on the parrot because even cardinal label cores contain large source deviations. The percentile deliberately trims their upper tail; it is an empirical heuristic, not a confidence bound for JPEG boundaries or rare correlated variation. No percentile sweep was used to select the result. Noise-zero tracing and components without a usable reference remain unchanged.

## Paint mechanism ledger

**Observed-sample medoid filter - rejected.** Choose an existing neighborhood RGB minimizing spatially weighted distance, preserving supported lines, to avoid inventing intermediate colors. Original midpoint squares and one-pixel outlines are controls. It retains high-noise midpoint squares that baseline loses and fixes the uncompressed sheet at noise 40, but default noise 10 changes nine paints/20 regions to ten/36. JPEG80 noise20/40 reaches 274/279 regions versus baseline 108/150; JPEG60 noise40 reaches 14 paints/400 regions versus 11/286. A single sheet run takes 3186 ms versus 1253 ms, an uncontrolled timing. Accent-versus-artifact ambiguity is not itself evidence of regression; the compressed-input fragmentation is.

**Least-variance directional filter - rejected.** Average only along the lowest-variance three-pixel line through the center to avoid crossing edges. The same accent/outline controls survive, and the high-noise uncompressed sheet improves, but default noise10 gives ten paints/33 regions. JPEG80 noise20/40 gives 210/210 regions and JPEG60 noise40 gives 14 paints/487 regions. Directional smoothing does not generalize across these resampling and compression conditions.

**Original-pixel detail discovery - rejected.** Run directional recovery and modal paint correction on unfiltered pixels while keeping the filtered main palette. This prevents the sheet's artificial plateau but adds recovered paints under JPEG80 noise20/40, reaching 12 instead of ten. It exposes codec structure to the same recovery rules that denoising was meant to stabilize.

**Original-pixel assignment - rejected.** Keep the filtered palette but use source RGB for nearest-paint and mixture decoding. JPEG fragmentation increases, and the diagnostic touching-junction explicit/noise40 case gains an exact fallback. Recovering source edge variation at assignment does not repair palette admission.

**Original-pixel boundary sampling - rejected as a global replacement.** Keep paint inference unchanged but fit local coverage from source RGB. Some rotated-cap and spiral measurements improve, but the diagnostic JPEG curve's maximum matched contour offset worsens by 0.25 pixels with lower ownership accuracy. The generated night image at noise40 gains an exact fallback. This changes contour sampling rather than false-paint inference and lacks a safe general improvement.

**Strict source-flat core corroboration - rejected.** Count a filtered 3x3 core only when all original neighbors also lie within the directional color gate. It removes the sheet's noise40 shade, but a 192-case control grid finds 14 losses of baseline-preserved noisy 4x4 and 6x6 midpoint accents. The grid crosses sizes 4/6/8, channel jitter 0/4/8/12, isolated/boundary placement, four noise levels, and Auto/explicit 3. The recipe is `noisyMidpointAccent` in `campaign-paints.js`. A core must tolerate noise inside a real paint.

**Source-gradient structure gate - rejected.** Use the local source gradient tensor to distinguish an edge-like plateau from isotropic variation, allowing truly flat cores. A version affecting new-paint promotion preserves the independent-channel accent controls but merely replaces false shades on `a.jpg`, reaching 67/66 regions at noise20/40 with three exact fallbacks at noise40. An independent correlated-noise challenge loses six baseline-preserved 4x4 accents under amplitude-eight column jitter at noise10: target recall falls from one to zero. Directed source variation can also be noise inside a real accent.

**Source-centroid recoloring - rejected.** Locate support in filtered pixels but estimate each new paint from the original support-site centroid, using core samples when at least four exist. It preserves the 192 accent controls and fixes the sheet, but `a.jpg` noise20 gains four exact fallbacks and 42 regions versus 34. In the independent blank split with amplitude-16 column jitter/noise10, it increases three paints/seven regions to five/13 with two recovered shades. Altering colors during discovery changes which later fringes are admitted.

**Source-centroid verification before assignment - rejected.** Keep discovery ordering and filtered colors, then delete a new paint when its source-site centroid matches a base palette paint under the existing separation criteria. Validating the batch avoids admitting an alternate fringe, preserves the `a.jpg` highlight and passes the first 1,440-configuration correlated matrix. An independent column-noise challenge nevertheless exposes a material regression: deleting a shade before mixture decoding changes seed evidence for other pixels, adds false vertical bands and moves the main interface by one pixel. Four reproducible controls in `campaign-paints.js` and `denoise-paints.test.js` reject this implementation. Fewer discovered paints do not establish preserved ownership or geometry.

**Relative source-distance corroboration - not selected.** Additionally require the source centroid to be closer to a qualifying base paint than to the proposed filtered paint. The Oklab variant restores the four correlated-column counterexamples byte-for-byte to baseline and preserves the 192 noisy-accent outcomes and 24 slit controls, but restores the resized sheet/noise40 to ten paints/236 regions and loses the `a.jpg` and JPEG improvements. The RGB variant retains the clean-sheet and slit improvement and avoids the four regressions, but still loses the `a.jpg` and JPEG benefits. These are distance-gate variants, not separate algorithm mechanisms. Neither addresses the demonstrated change in mixture-decoding context.
