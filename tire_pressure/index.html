Cargo & weight distribution

A preset is only an estimate. Front/rear cargo is modeled directly above that axle. Overhanging baskets, child seats and unusual geometries need measured wheel loads and equipment-specific advice.

Measure with rider and all luggage aboard, in a normal riding position, with both wheels at the same height. Use two scales, or one scale and an equal-height block. Have a helper steady the bike without taking weight. These readings replace all rider, bike and cargo entries.

2 · Tires & wheels

Use actual inflated casing width when possible, not the tread knobs. Rim width already affects that measurement; it is not counted again.

In "47–622", 47 is nominal width and 622 is bead-seat diameter. Inch wheel names can be ambiguous.

Setup notes

Assumptions & adjustments

Dial it in on your own bike

  1. Check the real limits.

    Use the lower tire/rim maximum and any specified minimum. Check tire compatibility, wheel and bicycle load ratings, and tire condition. A pressure calculator cannot approve a wheel-and-tire combination.

  2. Use a gauge, then test gently.

    Check cold pressure before the ride with a consistent gauge. Make small changes of about 5% within the permitted range. Never deliberately ride into a rim strike to find a lower limit.

  3. Adjust for feel and control.

    Harsh vibration may justify less pressure. Squirm, bottoming or air loss require stopping, checking for damage and reassessing pressure or equipment. Recheck whenever the load or tires change.

Pressure loss varies with the tube, tire, sealant, temperature and time. There is no reliable universal weekly loss rate. Schwalbe guidance · ENVE guidance

The formula, and what it cannot tell you

A load-and-width model, calibrated to published charts

base_bar = 4.53 × (wheel_load_kg / 42.5)
                 × (28 / actual_width_mm)^1.5
                 × √((622 + 2 × width) / (BSD + 2 × width))

tubeless: factor = surface_factor
with tube: factor = max(surface_factor, 1.00)

estimate_bar = base_bar × factor × impact_allowance
impact_allowance = 1.00, or 1.10 when selected

The model is linear in each wheel's static load and continuous in tire width. The width exponent and wheel correction are inspired by fixed-relative-deflection contact geometry, not a proof of optimal pressure or a claim of exactly 15% tire drop. The 4.53 bar coefficient was fitted to manufacturer charts. The tube floor and impact allowance are explicitly practical heuristics.

Surface factors in model 1.0
SurfaceFactorBasis

No automatic e-bike surcharge. Enter the actual bicycle mass, including motor and battery. Bike presets only fill visible settings. There is no mandatory rear-pressure offset: front cargo or different tire widths can reverse the usual relationship.

Unknown construction is a real limitation. Casing stiffness, tire inserts, rim support, speed, cornering, temperature, suspension, riding technique and tread can matter. They are not assigned invented precise coefficients here. A measured inflated width captures much of a rim-width change, but not tire/rim compatibility or all sidewall support effects.

Working range, not certified coverage: 23–75 mm tires, listed 305–635 mm bead-seat diameters, 45–160 kg total mass, and 10–110 kg per wheel. The principal calibration is much narrower: 25–61 mm, mostly about 65–125 kg total, on 622 mm wheels. Small-wheel results, heavy loads, ordinary inner tubes, and city/touring use have weaker direct support. Excluded: tubulars, fat bikes, tandems, heavy cargo bikes, downhill/jumps, velodromes and grip-limited racing cyclocross.

Research behind the estimates

Development included a review of 16 reference pages and charts covering tire pressure, rolling resistance, wheel loading and tire/rim compatibility.

The model was calibrated using 42 chart entries. Another 28 entries were held back to check the estimates, and 10 additional comparisons covered different contexts. Missing bicycle weights and load splits were recorded as assumptions.

These comparisons check agreement with published guidance. No riding or tire-deflection tests were performed for this tool, so they do not establish an ideal pressure for every bicycle. Equipment limits still apply.
