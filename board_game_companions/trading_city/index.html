Set up the table

Prepare your own game one task at a time. This guide uses the classic 110-card base game, without expansions.

Step 1 of 5 3 players · Standard opening

Choose the first Governor

Choose the first Governor before dealing any cards. This identifies the first seat for the opening-hand variant too.

  • Agree on a starting player and give that player the Governor placard.
  • Lay all 5 role placards face up in the middle, within reach of everyone.
  • Seat order is clockwise. The Governor is a starting-player marker, not a sixth selectable role.
  • Keep a scoring pad and pencil nearby for the end of the game.

Prepare the trading house

The trading house determines sale prices. Prepare its stack now; leave the prices hidden.

  • Shuffle all 5 trading house tiles face down.
  • Stack them face down beside the role placards.
  • Reveal the top tile only when a selling phase begins.

After a selling phase, put the revealed tile face down under the stack. Do not reshuffle it or change it just because a new round starts.

Place the starting buildings

Before shuffling the building cards, find one Indigo plant for each player.

  • Place that card face up in its owner's play area. It is already built, so no payment is needed.
  • Start with no goods on it. Do not take a face-down goods card yet.
  • Leave space for a growing city. The starting building already counts toward 12 buildings.

Deal the opening hands

Shuffle all remaining building cards into a face-down card supply. Keep a separate space for a face-down discard stack.

Deal 4 cards from the supply to each player. Everyone keeps all four, without paying for them.

In clockwise order from the Governor, deal 5, 6, 7, or 8 cards according to seat. Each player keeps exactly 4 and discards the rest face down.

Keep card faces private, but make the number of cards in your hand known. Hand cards are both your building choices and your spending money.

Check the first round

Check the table together before the Governor chooses the first role.

  • Each player has 1 face-up Indigo plant, 4 hand cards, and no goods.
  • All 5 roles are available, and the trading house stack is face down.
  • The supply and discard area are easy to tell apart. Both use face-down cards.
  • Do not pass the Governor or deal another hand before the first action.

When a role is chosen, read its action and privilege aloud. Learn its details from the physical placard when it comes up; there is no role-by-role lesson here.

The table is ready

3 players · 4 cards and 1 building each

The Governor chooses first. Everyone resolves the chosen phase before the next player selects a role. Twelve buildings trigger the end; victory points decide the winner.

Learn the core rules

Learn the core rules

Start with what a card can do, then follow a round, pay for a building, handle goods, and score. Use the actual role placards for their individual actions and privileges.

One card, three uses

Your aim is to finish with the most victory points, not the most cards or the fastest twelfth building. A card's location determines what it does.

In your hand
A possible building, or one card of payment when discarded. Its printed cost does not make it worth more money.
Face up in your city
A built building. Its type, ability, and victory points matter here. Simply holding its card gives no ability.
Face down on a production building
One good of that building's type. Do not inspect the hidden card or use its printed text.
A card can do only one of these jobs at a time.

The card you build stays in your city. The other cards you spend go to the discard stack. A goods card is not part of your hand.

Check your understanding
You pay with a hand card showing a building cost of 6. How much payment does it provide?

Choose a role; resolve a phase

  1. 1 · ChooseThe Governor chooses first. Take an available role placard; it stays with you until the round ends.
  2. 2 · ResolveThe chooser acts first, with the privilege. The other players follow clockwise with the action only.
  3. 3 · Next choiceAfter the phase, the next chooser is the player to the left of the previous chooser. Use a different available role.
  4. 4 · New roundAfter the required selections, return all roles and pass the Governor clockwise. Do the start-of-round checks.

Choosing is mandatory; acting is optional. You may skip an action or privilege without cancelling the phase for others. Skipping does not stop you taking part in later phases.

Shared-action exception: the Prospector has no general action for the other players. Relevant building effects can still apply. Read the privilege from the placard.

Role selection at your table

    Before the next round's first choice

    Return the role placards and pass the Governor. Resolve any Chapel use first: its owner may put one hand card beneath it. Then discard down to 7 hand cards, or 12 with a Tower. Buildings and goods stay in place; there is no automatic draw.

    Check your understanding
    The player who selected a role skips the action. What happens to the shared action?

    Pay with other hand cards

    During a Builder phase, each player may normally build one building. Start with the chooser, then proceed clockwise.

    1. Choose a building from your hand. Check that it is legal and that you can afford it.
    2. Calculate its cost after applicable discounts. The Builder's own privilege reduces payment by 1; discounts cannot make the cost negative.
    3. Place the chosen building face up. Discard the required number of other hand cards face down as payment.
    4. Resolve applicable effects from buildings you already had. A new violet building's ability starts only after this Builder phase.
    Example: cost 3, no discounts

    You need the building card plus 3 other hand cards: 4 cards in all. Keep the building and discard the other 3. A payment card printed with cost 6 still counts as just one card.

    Production buildings
    Several of the same type are allowed in one city. Each has room for just one good.
    Violet buildings
    No two copies of the same violet building in your city. Different players may own the same building.
    No change
    A cost reduced to zero means no payment cards are discarded. Unused discounts do not give you cards.
    Check your understanding
    You hold 4 cards in total and want to build a cost-4 building with no discount. Can you build it?

    Turn goods into new hand cards

    Production and selling happen only when their roles are chosen. Their order is not fixed: a round does not automatically include either phase.

    Producing goods

    The basic production action allows one good. For each good you may produce, put one card from the supply, unseen and face down, on an empty production building you own. Even with extra production, a building holds at most one good.

    Selling goods

    The basic selling action allows one good; the chosen role's privilege and your buildings may allow more. Reveal one trading house tile for the entire phase. Choose the goods to sell, discard those goods cards unseen, and draw new hand cards equal to their total sale price. Leave the production buildings in place.

    Supply → buildingThe face-down card becomes a good.
    Building determines the typeThe hidden card's face never sets the price.
    Sold good → discardThe building is empty and usable again.
    Sale income → handDraw new cards; do not keep the sold goods card.

    After everyone has had a chance to sell, put the trading house tile face down under its stack—even if nobody sold anything. Players may sell the same type of good; one player's sale does not block another's.

    Drawing and keeping cards

    For a card-drawing action, use the draw and keep amounts on the chosen placard. Keep that new selection separate from your old hand until you have chosen what to keep, unless a building allows mixing them. Discard the rest face down. There is no general refill to four or seven cards.

    Supply empty? Shuffle the discard stack into a new face-down supply and continue drawing. Emptying the supply does not end the game.

    Check your understanding
    You sell one good worth 3 cards. Which cards go into your hand?

    Finish the phase, then score

    12 buildings end the game—not necessarily with a win for their owner.

    When at least one player has twelve buildings, finish that entire Builder phase. Players who have not acted in it still get their opportunity. Stop afterward; do not finish the round.

    The starting Indigo plant counts as a building. Hand cards and goods do not. If an effect has covered an older building, the covered card is not an extra building.

    1 · Printed victory points
    Add the victory-point values of your built buildings, not their construction costs.
    2 · Chapel cards
    Add 1 point per card beneath your Chapel, if you have one. Ignore the hidden faces.
    3 · Other end-game bonuses
    Calculate bonuses from your scoring buildings using their own text. Do not assume every cost-6 card is worth 6 points.
    4 · Palace last
    If you own a Palace, add one quarter of your other points, rounded down. Do not include its own bonus in that calculation.

    Most victory points wins. Among tied players, compare hand cards + unsold goods, counting each as one regardless of sale price. These cards are a tie-break, not extra victory points.

    Check your understanding
    Another player reaches twelve buildings before your action in this Builder phase. May you still build?

    Rules reference

    Open a topic when it comes up. Building text can change these defaults. This is a core-rules companion, not a catalogue of roles or building powers.

    Rounds and timing

    Role selection and skipping

    A role must be chosen when it is your turn to choose, even if you will not use it. The action and privilege are optional. A selected placard stays unavailable for the rest of the round. Resolve the phase before the next choice; there is no fixed order of phases. The privilege belongs only to the chooser.

    Two-player round

    The role selectors are Governor → opponent → Governor: three different roles in total. Each selected phase is resolved normally. Return the roles only after the third phase, then pass the Governor; the other player will choose twice next round.

    A Library may be used only once per round in the two-player game, not once for each of the Governor's two choices.

    When to enforce the hand limit

    Only at the start of a new round: use the Chapel first if desired, then discard to 7 cards, or 12 with a Tower. Having more during a round is allowed. Reveal your hand count, not your card faces. There is no automatic draw when a round starts or when your hand is empty.

    Example: you draw up to 9 cards during a round. Keep them for now; a later action may spend some before the next hand-limit check.

    Cards, costs, and goods

    Building, payment, and activation

    One building per player in a Builder phase is the default. Its card is separate from its payment. Each other hand card pays one unit, whatever is printed on it. Apply valid reductions, with a minimum cost of zero. No duplicate violet buildings within one city; duplicate production buildings are allowed. A newly built violet ability cannot help with its own construction or any part of that same Builder phase.

    Goods are not hand cards

    A goods card comes unseen from the supply and sits face down on an empty production building. One building holds one good. The building determines the good's type. Goods may remain between rounds. Selling removes the goods card, not the building; the income is newly drawn hand cards. Neither production nor selling gives victory points as its normal action.

    Trading house and card supply

    Reveal one trading house tile when a selling phase starts. Everyone uses it, including players selling the same type of good. After the phase, move it face down to the bottom even if no goods were sold. Do not reveal a new tile merely at a round change. When the card supply empties, shuffle the face-down discard stack to replace it.

    Ending and scope

    The twelfth building and tied scores

    Finish the Builder phase that leaves at least one city with twelve buildings, then score immediately. Do not complete the round or enforce a new hand limit. Count printed building points, Chapel cards, other scoring bonuses, and the Palace bonus last. Highest score wins; tied players compare the total number of hand cards plus goods. The base rules specify no further tie-break if that is equal too.

    Optional opening-hand variant

    Standard setup gives four random hand cards to everyone. The optional variant deals 5 to the Governor, then 6, 7, and 8 clockwise where those seats exist. Everyone keeps four and discards the surplus face down. The starting Indigo plant is separate in both methods.

    Guide scope and independence

    Rules baseline: the original 110-card base game. Expansion buildings, events, and later rethemed editions are outside this guide. For an individual role, read your role placard; for a special building, consult its text and your edition's rulebook.
