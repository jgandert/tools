Set up the table

Base game · 2–5 players · original player mats. Prepare one part of the table at a time, then learn the shared rules without studying every bird.

Step 1 of 53 players · Blue · points per item

Prepare the shared supply

Use the base-game components and the original player mats. This guide uses normal random setup, not a pre-arranged quick-start tutorial.

  • Shuffle the bird cards. Put 3 face up on the bird tray; keep the remaining deck face down beside it.
  • Shuffle the bonus cards into a separate face-down deck. Leave space for both discard piles.
  • Put the food tokens and egg miniatures within reach. Egg colors have no rules meaning.
  • Roll all 5 food dice into the birdfeeder. Leave the scorepad nearby for the end of the game.

Choose the round goals

Choose one side of the goal board for the whole game. The guide option must match the physical board on your table.

The blue side awards points for your own result. The green side compares everyone's results and awards points by rank.

  • Mix the goal tiles without choosing their faces. Place 1 random tile, random side up, in each of the 4 round spaces.
  • Return unused goal tiles to the box. Read the four visible goals together before choosing your starting cards.
  • Do not place an action cube on the goal board yet. You will use one after each round, even for a score of zero.

Give everyone a player kit

Every player starts with the same quantities, but different random cards.

  • Give each player 1 player mat and 8 action cubes of one color. Leave all habitat spaces empty.
  • Deal 5 bird cards and 2 bonus cards to each player. These are choices, not the final starting hand.
  • Give each player 5 food tokens: 1 invertebrate, 1 seed, 1 fish, 1 fruit and 1 rodent.
  • Do not take any eggs. Keep food beside the mat, not on bird cards.

Choose birds, food and one bonus

  • Keep any 0–5 of your dealt bird cards. Discard the others.
  • For every bird card you keep, return 1 of your starting food tokens to the supply. You choose which food to return.
  • Keep all remaining food. Your kept bird cards plus remaining food tokens total 5.
  • Choose 1 of your 2 bonus cards; discard the other. This choice does not cost food.

Keeping is not playing. These bird cards stay in your hand. Their printed food cost and any column egg cost are paid later, when you play them.

Choose a first player and begin

Randomly choose a first player and give them the first-player token. Turns proceed clockwise.

  • The first player spends 1 action cube to choose exactly 1 of the four actions. Each other player then does the same on their turn.
  • A turn is one player's action. A round ends only after everyone has spent all available action cubes.
  • In round 1, each player has 8 turns. Later rounds give each player 7, then 6, then 5 turns.
  • Do not roll the food dice before every turn. Follow the birdfeeder refill and reroll rules instead.

Table is ready

Everyone has 8 unused action cubes, an empty mat, one bonus card, and a starting mix of 5 bird cards and food tokens combined. Nobody starts with eggs.

Learn the first turn

Learn the game

Start with the turn, then learn playing birds, habitat actions, power timing and scoring. Each lesson can be opened independently at the table.

Choose one action, then finish the turn

Build your bird collection over four rounds and finish with the most points. Birds can improve a habitat's basic action and add powers when that habitat is used.

One turn = one action cube = one chosen action.

The four choices are alternatives, not four steps you must perform in order. You may choose the same action again on a later turn.

Play a Bird
Add a bird from your hand, pay its costs and use its "When Played" power, if it has one. This does not activate the habitat.

Take food using birdfeeder dice, then activate brown powers in your forest from right to left.
Lay Eggs · grassland
Place eggs on your birds, then activate brown powers in your grassland from right to left.
Draw Bird Cards · wetland
Take cards into your hand, then activate brown powers in your wetland from right to left.

The shared pattern for habitat actions

  1. Choose a rowPut 1 unused cube on its leftmost uncovered action space. With 5 birds, use the printed space at the far right.
  2. Take the basic benefitUse only that space. Its printed extra exchange is optional and may be used once.
  3. Move right to leftPass each bird in that row. You may activate each brown power when you reach it; skip other colors.
  4. Leave the cube on the leftRefill empty bird-tray spaces only after the whole turn. The cube remains used until round end.
Check: can I gain food and also play a bird with my one action cube?

Not as two normal actions. Choose one. A bird power can grant an extra effect, but only when its text says so.

Play a bird and pay the right costs

A bird must be in your hand before you play it. Check its permitted habitats, the next empty space in that row, and whether you can pay the full food and egg costs.

  1. Choose one habitat printed on the bird card. It must have room, and the bird goes in that row's leftmost empty slot.
  2. Place an unused action cube above the column you will use. Return the required eggs from any of your birds to the supply.
  3. Pay the bird's food cost using tokens beside your mat. Put the bird in its chosen slot and move the cube to the left of the Play a Bird row.
  4. You may use its white "When Played" power now. Do not activate brown powers just because a bird was played.

The egg cost depends on the column in the chosen habitat, not on your total number of birds. Eggs can come from different birds in different habitats.

Food costs: wild, "or", no food and the 2-for-1 substitution
  • A wild food symbol accepts any 1 food token. A slash means choose one of the shown types, not both. A crossed-out circle means no food is required; the column's egg cost still applies.
  • Only when paying to play a bird, any 2 food tokens can replace 1 required food token. The two tokens may be the same type or different types. Repeat for other missing food if needed.
  • This is a payment substitution, not a general trading action. It cannot replace eggs or a specific food cost on a bird power.

Read your physical bird card

Habitat and food cost
Where the bird can be played, and what food you pay. Multiple habitats give a choice, not multiple placements.
Point value
Printed points count at game end only for birds played on your mat.
Nest type and egg limit
The nest matters for some powers and goals. The egg limit is the maximum number of eggs that bird can hold.
wingspan (cm)
Used by some powers and bonus conditions; it is not an extra point value.
Power and timing
Read the timing label and the text. You do not need to learn every bird before starting.
Check: my third bird goes in the first column of an empty habitat. How many eggs?

Zero eggs. This is column 1 in that habitat, even though it is your third bird overall. Pay the printed food cost as usual.

Food, eggs and bird cards

For these three actions, the basic benefit comes first, then the brown powers in that habitat. The number of birds in other habitats does not change the chosen row's basic benefit.

Gain Food · forest

For each food die shown on your action space, remove 1 die from the birdfeeder and take 1 matching food token from the supply. Choose dice one at a time. The invertebrate/seed face gives either 1 invertebrate or 1 seed, never both.

On a space with a card-to-food exchange, you may discard 1 bird card from your hand to take 1 additional die and its matching food token. Then resolve the forest's brown powers.

When do we reroll the birdfeeder?

Empty: immediately roll all 5 dice back in. All remaining dice show exactly the same face: when you are about to gain food, you may first reroll all 5 dice. A single remaining die qualifies.

The split invertebrate/seed face is its own face. A seed face plus an invertebrate/seed face are not "all the same". Recheck between taking individual dice. A new turn or round is not, by itself, a reason to reroll.

Lay Eggs · grassland

Take the number of eggs shown on the action space and distribute them among any of your birds, in any habitats. Never exceed a bird's egg limit. Eggs with no available space are lost; they cannot wait beside your mat.

On a space with a food-to-egg exchange, you may return 1 food token from beside your mat for 1 additional egg. Then resolve the grassland's brown powers. Egg colors do not matter.

Draw Bird Cards · wetland

Draw the number of bird cards shown. For each card, choose a face-up card from the bird tray or the top card of the deck. On a space with an egg-to-card exchange, you may discard 1 egg from any of your birds to draw 1 extra card.

Wait to refill the bird tray until your entire turn ends, including all brown powers. There is no hand limit. If the deck runs out, shuffle the discarded bird cards into a new deck.

Read timing, not a catalogue of birds

Learn the three timing categories, then read the birds you actually play. Powers are optional, but a stated cost must be paid to receive the benefit linked to that cost.

When Played · white
Use once, as that bird enters your mat. It does not repeat on later habitat activations.
When Activated · brown
When you take the matching habitat action, resolve brown powers from the rightmost bird toward the left. You may skip a power; do not rearrange their order.
Once Between Turns · pink
Use on an opponent's turn when the stated trigger happens. Each pink power can be used at most once between your own turns, not once per opponent. It can also trigger once after your final turn, before the game ends.

Read where a power gains food or cards: "from the supply", "from the birdfeeder", "from the deck" and "from your hand" are different instructions. Taking a benefit is not automatically taking the corresponding action.

Food and cards can become points

Food beside your mat
Available to spend. You cannot freely move it onto a bird for points.
Cached food
Food placed on a bird by a power. Each token is worth 1 point at game end and is not normally available to spend.
Tucked cards
Cards placed face down under a bird by a power. Each counts as 1 point, not as another played bird. Ignore its printed points, habitat and power.

Cached food and tucked cards have no general quantity limit. They stay with their bird; you cannot take them back into your supply or hand unless a card explicitly allows it.

Check: two opponents trigger my pink power before my next turn. Can I use it twice?

No. That bird's pink power can be used only once in that interval. Other pink birds track their own limit separately. Tell the table what triggers your pink powers.

Nests and eggs: the star is wild, the egg limit is not

A star nest can match any nest type for a power, goal or bonus card. It does not increase the bird's egg limit. Egg-laying powers still respect capacity and any restrictions about which bird or nest receives the egg.

End a round, then score the game

After everyone has used their last action cube, finish the round together. The round goal checks what you have now, not everything you earned earlier in the round.

  1. Take the used action cubes off your mat. Use 1 to mark your result for the current round on the goal board, even if you scored 0.
  2. Leave all earlier goal cubes in place. The remaining cubes are available next round.
  3. Discard the face-up bird cards remaining in the bird tray and reveal 3 new ones. Keep your own birds, eggs, food and hand cards.
  4. After rounds 1–3, pass the first-player token clockwise. After round 4, score the game; there is no fifth round.

  2. Round 27 turns per player
  3. Round 36 turns per player
  4. Round 45 turns per player

Score the current round goal

Both goal-board sides and ties

Blue: score 1 point per qualifying item or complete set, up to 5 points. Other players' quantities do not reduce your score.

Green: rank positive quantities from highest to lowest. Use the printed points for the current round. A tie occupies consecutive places: total those points, divide by the number tied, then round down. Skip the places already occupied by the tie. Fourth and fifth place are worth 0.

Example: in round 3, the first three places are worth 6, 3 and 2 points. Two players tied for first receive 4 each: (6 + 3) ÷ 2, rounded down. The next player is third and receives 2, provided they have at least one qualifying item.

On either side, a result of zero still uses an action cube. Counting a bird or egg for a goal does not remove it from your mat.

Highest total wins. A tie is broken by the most unspent food tokens beside the mat, not cached food. If still tied, share the win.

Hand cards and unused food do not score by themselves. Apply any bonus card that specifically rewards your hand. You do not get a final action to spend leftover resources.

Rules reference

Open the rule you need. The defaults below assume the base game; follow the text of a card when it explicitly changes a rule.

At the table

Which action do I need?

New bird on the mat: Play a Bird. Food tokens: Gain Food in the forest. Eggs: Lay Eggs in the grassland. More hand cards: Draw Bird Cards in the wetland. The last three also activate that row's brown powers from right to left.

Empty rows, full rows and repeating actions

You may use a habitat with no birds: take its first printed benefit. With 5 birds, use its far-right action space and then the brown powers. You cannot normally add a sixth bird to that habitat. Played birds stay on your mat unless another bird's power removes one; there is no regular discard action. Used action cubes do not block spaces or stop you choosing that action on another turn.

What must I pay to play a bird?

Pay its printed food plus the column's egg cost: 0 / 1 / 1 / 2 / 2 in columns 1–5. The habitat must be permitted and the slot must be the leftmost empty one in that row. Two food tokens can replace one required food only for playing a bird. No substitute replaces an egg.

Birdfeeder: empty or identical dice

Empty: roll all 5 dice back in immediately. Identical remaining faces, including a lone die: you may reroll all 5 before gaining food. Check again between individual selections. The invertebrate/seed face is different from either single-food face. The same rules apply when a power lets you gain food from the birdfeeder.

When do the bird tray and deck refill?

Empty tray spaces refill at the end of the entire turn, not after each card or each bird power. Round end replaces all remaining face-up birds. An exhausted deck is rebuilt by shuffling discarded bird cards. There is no hand limit; cards you kept stay in hand between rounds.

Egg capacity, placement and spending

The grassland action can place eggs on your birds in any habitat. Never exceed an egg limit, even through a power. Egg colors are interchangeable. Eggs already placed cannot be freely moved between birds. When paying eggs, choose which of your birds lose them; return them to the shared supply.

Optional benefits are not optional costs

You may decline a benefit or skip a bird power. You may not claim a linked benefit without paying its required cost. Skipping a habitat's basic benefit does not prevent using its brown powers. An action still uses a cube even if you take fewer benefits or none.

Powers and points

Timing reminder

White: when that bird is played. Brown: when you use its habitat, in right-to-left order. Pink: on an opponent's triggering turn, at most once between your own turns for each such power, including once after your final turn before the game ends. Playing a bird does not run a habitat chain. Additional colored timing categories belong to expansions and are outside this guide.

Cached food is not your spendable food

Food beside your mat is spendable. Food cached on a bird is normally locked in for 1 point per token. Cards tucked under a bird are each 1 point, not extra birds or their printed values. You may cache food or tuck cards only when an effect permits it.

Read the goal's unit: birds, eggs or sets

A goal for birds with eggs counts each qualifying bird once, however many eggs it holds. An egg goal counts every qualifying egg. One set across habitats needs an egg in each of forest, grassland and wetland; the number of complete sets is the smallest of those three egg totals. A star nest can satisfy a required nest type.

Green ties, blue limits and zero results

Green: share the point values of all places occupied by tied players and round each share down; the next player skips those places. Blue: 1 point per qualifying item or set, maximum 5. With zero qualifying items you score zero on either side, but still leave an action cube on the goal board.

What survives round end?

Keep birds, eggs, hand cards, bonus cards, cached food, tucked cards and unused food. Return the used cubes from your mat, then leave one on the current goal. Keep earlier goal cubes there too. Replace the tray's birds and pass first player. Do not automatically reroll the birdfeeder or feed your birds.

Final scoring and the tie-break

After round 4, add bird points, bonus-card points, round-goal points, eggs, cached food and tucked cards. Each of the last three is worth 1 point per item. Unused food beside the mat breaks a tied total; it is not otherwise worth points. A remaining tie is a shared win.

Scope and independence

About this independent guide

This is an independent teaching companion for the base game designed by Elizabeth Hargrave, for 2–5 players using the original player mats. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Stonemaier Games or Feuerland Spiele. You need your own game components.

The explanations and practice examples are newly written. No publisher artwork, logos, card images, card catalogue or full card texts are included. Rules terminology is retained so the guide matches the physical game.

Standard random setup, basic actions, the three base-game power timings, round goals and final scoring are covered. Automa, expansions, changed player mats and the pre-arranged quick-start tutorial are not included. Card-specific exceptions are outside this guide; follow the text on your cards.
