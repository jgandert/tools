Set up the table

For the 3–5 player base game. Choose your table settings, then complete one task at a time. The goal is five foreign colonies—not five ships.

Step 1 of 6 4 players · First play · no flares

Make room for five foreign colonies

Put the warp in the middle and the hyperspace gate beside it. Choose a different player color for each person.

  • Take five planets, twenty ships, and one colony marker in your color.
  • Your planets form your home system. Every other system is foreign to you.
  • Leave space for the cosmic deck, destiny deck, and their separate face-up discard piles.

To win, maintain colonies on five different foreign planets. They can all be in the same foreign system.

Learn the rules

Start with the goal, walk through an encounter, then see how cards and outcomes fit together. No individual alien powers are taught here.

Count planets, not ships

Your target is five foreign colonies at the same time. One or more of your ships on a planet is one colony. More ships make that colony larger, not more valuable. Several players can each have a colony on the same planet.

Know your role in this encounter

Offense
The player whose turn it is. Sends ships to the hyperspace gate.
Defense
The opponent determined by destiny. Normally the owner of the targeted system, even with no ships on the target planet.
Main players
The offense and defense. Only they choose the facedown encounter cards.
Allies
Other players who accept an invitation and commit ships to one side.
Bystanders
Players not taking part in this encounter. Their uninvolved ships already on the target planet add no strength and are not normally harmed.

Colonies are not permanent points. Remove your final ship and you lose the colony immediately. The foreign colony track can go down as well as up.

Before the first encounter: Start Turn

Check your hand as the offense. With no encounter card (Attack, Negotiate, or Morph), reveal and discard the remaining hand and draw eight new cards. Repeat if necessary. Do this once at the start of your turn—not before the second encounter.

Which edition does this guide teach?

This guide separates Start Turn from the seven encounter phases, as in the classic rulebook. The revised English book numbers Start Turn as an eighth phase. A second encounter still starts at Regroup.

Phase 1 of 7

Three encounter cards; other cards are extra effects

Attack
A number for comparing strength against another Attack. Each participating ship normally adds one.
Negotiate
A chance to make a deal if both players choose it. Against Attack, you lose and claim compensation for your own ships lost to the warp.
Morph
Copies the opponent's revealed encounter card. It does not copy allies, ships, or powers.

These are not encounter cards

Reinforcement
After reveal, a main player or ally may add its printed bonus to either side. Multiple reinforcements can be played at legal timing. Discard them after the encounter.
Artifact
A one-use effect with its own role and timing restrictions. Resolve its text and discard it. An observer can play one only if the card's conditions permit it.
Flare
An optional layer for later games. A flare normally returns to hand after use. Each player may play at most one flare per encounter; the same flare may be used only once in that encounter, even if it changes hands.

With a matching active alien, use the flare's Super effect; otherwise use Wild. Losing your alien power, including a zap, prevents the matching Super effect. Holding a flare does not give you that alien's power.

Reading timing, not memorizing every card

Check who may play the card, in which phase, and exactly what triggers it. "At any time" still does not interrupt an effect already resolving unless the card specifically permits that interruption.

Cosmic Zap stops a use of an alien power for the encounter; it does not cancel every card. Card Zap cancels an artifact or flare as it is played—not an Attack, Negotiate, Morph, or Reinforcement. Follow your physical cards for exact conditions.

Follow the result to the right reward

Default outcomes after cards and effects resolve
Result Ships and benefits
Offense wins Offense and offensive allies land all committed ships. Each can establish a colony; an existing colony is only reinforced. Defense and defensive allies lose participating ships to the warp.
Defense wins All gate ships go to the warp. The defender stays. Defensive allies take rewards and return to existing colonies, not a new colony on the defended planet.
Successful deal Allies return and get nothing. Main players carry out the agreed card transfers and/or colony grants. Any remaining gate ships return.
Failed deal Allies return safely. Each main player chooses three of their own ships from colonies or the gate to lose to the warp. Other gate ships return.

Defender rewards

Only winning defensive allies receive these. For each ship committed, choose one card from the cosmic deck or retrieve one own ship from the warp. Choices can be mixed. With three allied ships, two cards plus one retrieved ship is a valid selection.

Compensation

Only the losing main player who revealed Negotiate against Attack receives it. Take random cards from the opposing main player's hand: one for each of your own ships actually lost to the warp in that defeat, limited to the cards available. Allied losses never increase your compensation, and allies receive none.

A defensive win keeps a colony; it does not create a new one. Offensive allies can share a win with the offense if both still hold five foreign colonies at the end of Resolution.

A deal must change something tangible

At least one card must transfer or one colony must be established. Either side may give cards, and each main player can gain at most one colony, on a planet where the other already has a colony. The host's ships remain. Ships for the new colony come from existing colonies or the gate, not the warp. Allies cannot be included. Promises alone are not a valid deal.

Resolve the agreed card and colony terms honestly. A promise of future help is not enforceable. With no valid deal within one minute, each main player loses three ships.

A turn is not the same as an encounter

  1. Start Turn
    Check the offense's encounter cards once.
  2. First encounter
    Play from Regroup through Resolution.
  3. Optional second
    After success, with an encounter card left, start again at Regroup.
  4. Pass clockwise
    After failure, declining a second, or completing a second.

A successful first encounter can be a win, a valid deal, or reclaiming a completely empty home planet after drawing your own color. A successful deal does not count as a battle win for effects that specifically require a win.

There is no routine card draw at turn end. A low hand is normal. Allies do not replace their hand just because they have no encounter cards.

How to use your chosen alien—not a catalogue

Read its role restriction, highlighted timing, and whether the effect is mandatory or optional. Apply any setup instructions. Its specified exceptions override the relevant general rule; they do not suspend unrelated rules.

If you have colonies on fewer than three of your five home planets, turn the alien sheet facedown and lose the power. Foreign colonies do not protect it. Regain a third home colony and the power returns immediately. With no colonies, you remain in the game.

When effects compete

Respect each effect's exact trigger. If effects truly compete at the same timing, use offense first, defense second, then other players clockwise from the offense. Finish an effect before another begins unless an explicit interrupt, such as a suitable zap, applies.

In a teaching demonstration you can set powers aside briefly to show the baseline. That is an exercise—not a claim that powers are optional in an actual game.

Colonies, movement, and victory

One or more ships of your color on a planet is one colony. Only foreign colonies count toward five; several can be in one system. The count reflects colonies currently held, not all colonies ever acquired. Unless an effect says otherwise, victory is checked at the very end of Resolution: whoever still holds five foreign colonies wins; if more than one player reaches five at once, they share the win. Returning ships go to existing colonies only, unless an effect expressly establishes a new one. Removing your last ship loses that colony immediately.

Destiny, your own color, and empty planets

Another color names the defending player and their home system; the offense chooses a planet there. Wild and special cards state their selection rule. With your own color, redraw or challenge a foreign colony on a home planet, choosing exactly one defending player; other colonies are bystanders. Alternatively, reclaim a completely empty home planet with 1–4 ships. This ends after Launch and counts as a successful encounter. A foreign player's empty home planet is still defended with a card and possible allies.

Alliances, bystanders, and ship limits

Offense invites before defense. Starting left of the offense, players accept in clockwise order and each commits before the next answers. One invitation may be accepted per player; declining is allowed. Offense and each ally normally commit 1–4 ships. The defense uses all their ships already on the target planet, even more than four; no extra ships may be moved there from elsewhere. Bystander ships are uninvolved unless explicitly committed as allied ships. A player can be an ally without holding an encounter card. Defensive allies count as being in the hyperspace gate.

New hands are not top-ups

At Start Turn, an offense without an encounter card reveals and discards the rest of the hand and draws eight, repeating if necessary. Players may play cards that are legal before a new-hand draw, but they are not required to. A defense without an encounter card replaces their hand when required to choose one. Applicable pre-draw effects must respect their printed timing. Offense does not replace a hand between encounters and cannot take a second encounter without an encounter card. If offense loses all encounter cards before selection during an encounter, end the turn and return committed ships. A selected final encounter card still resolves. Allies get no automatic replacement. There is no standard hand-size limit or turn-end draw.

Defender rewards are for defensive allies

When the defense wins, each defensive ally gets one reward per ship committed: either one cosmic-deck card or one own ship retrieved from the warp to an existing colony. Mix the choices freely. Return the committed allied ships to existing colonies; do not establish a new colony. The defending main player gets no reward. Offensive allies gain colonies when they win, not defender rewards. Allies returning because both sides negotiated get nothing.

Compensation counts your own losses only

A main player who loses with Negotiate against Attack takes random cards from the opposing main player's remaining hand. Count their own ships actually sent to the warp by this defeat, not allies and not prevented losses. If fewer cards remain, take only those. Defensive allies do not share or pay compensation. Reinforcements do not make Negotiate win against Attack.

Deals: sixty seconds and a real exchange

Both main players resolving Negotiate return allied ships to existing colonies without rewards. Within one minute agree on a hand-card transfer and/or up to one new colony for each main player. A colony may be granted only on a planet where the grantor has a colony; the grantor need not leave. Ships may come from the recipient's colonies or gate, not the warp. At least one card or colony must change; a one-sided deal is legal. Agreed card and colony terms must be carried out honestly; future promises are unenforceable. Failure costs each main player three own ships, chosen from colonies or gate. Return remaining gate ships. Do not charge allies.

When can I have a second encounter?

Only after a successful first encounter, and only with an encounter card still in hand. Winning, making a valid deal, or reclaiming a completely empty home planet with your own destiny color is success. The second encounter is optional and starts at Regroup: retrieve another ship if available and draw a new destiny card. Start Turn does not repeat, so there is no new-hand replacement. After the second encounter, pass clockwise regardless of its outcome. A failed first encounter ends the turn immediately.

Power loss, restoration, and timing

Read only the alien you chose. Check role, timing, mandatory/optional status, and setup. Its specified exception overrides the corresponding general rule. Fewer than three home colonies disables the alien power; flip the sheet facedown. Restoring a third home colony immediately restores it. Foreign colonies do not count for this test. When simultaneous effects compete, resolve the offense first, then the defense, then other players starting left of the offense and continuing clockwise; effects with no associated player resolve last. Finish each effect before the next unless a rule allows an interrupt. A Cosmic Zap cancels power use for the encounter, not all text on every card.

Flares, artifacts, reinforcements, and zaps

Only Attack, Negotiate, and Morph are encounter cards. Reinforcements may be played after reveal by main players or allies for either side, even in a negotiation; they do not change card type. Artifacts are discarded after use. Flares normally return to hand. One flare per player per encounter, and each particular flare only once per encounter, even after changing hands. A matching active alien uses Super; otherwise Wild. Losing or zapping that power prevents Super. Card Zap targets an artifact or flare as it is played; it cannot cancel an encounter or reinforcement card. Use printed card text for exceptions.

Empty decks and the cosmic quake

Keep the two decks separate. With one destiny card left, shuffle that last card together with destiny discards before drawing. When the cosmic deck runs out, shuffle its discards into a new deck. If a cosmic card must be drawn and both that deck and its discard pile are empty, a cosmic quake makes all players discard their hands, shuffle those cards into a new cosmic deck, and receive eight cards each. Do not trigger this just because a deck looks low.

No colonies or no attacking ships

You are not eliminated for losing all colonies. During your Regroup, the recovered ship goes straight into the gate if you have no colonies. Other ships unable to return to any colony normally go to the warp. If the offense cannot supply any ships or loses its last attacking ship to an effect, the encounter still continues; winning without a ship does not give that player a colony. The practice calculator models the usual 1–4 ship launch rather than this exceptional case.

Encounter cards, totals, and ties

Attack against Attack compares each side's card value plus its own participating ships, allied ships, and applicable bonuses. The higher total wins; a tied total goes to the defense. Attack defeats Negotiate without comparing totals. Two Negotiate cards lead to a deal attempt. Morph becomes the opponent's encounter card, not their ships, allies, or power: Morph against Attack 10 is another Attack 10; Morph against Negotiate is another Negotiate. Main players and allies may add reinforcements to either side after reveal at legal timing; bystanders may not. Reinforcements do not change which encounter-card types were revealed.

English–German terminology

German component names follow the German FFG / Heidelberger rulebook, not literal translations. In particular, the Regroup phase is "Verstärkung"; Reinforcement cards are "Verstärkungskarten."

Edition, scope, and independence

Which edition does this guide teach?

The multiplayer FFG base rules, with the German FFG / Heidelberger terminology. Not the separate two-player game or older non-FFG editions. Setup uses the two-flare alien selection procedure shared by the classic rules and the revised book's advanced setup. First play limits that draw to green-alert aliens and leaves flares out afterward.

Newer boxes may also supply combo cards: these provide a preset alien group for the chosen player count. They are an alternative setup, not required by this guide. The revised beginner setup also lets the youngest player start the very first game; this guide uses the classic destiny draw.

This guide separates Start Turn from the seven encounter phases, as in the classic rulebook. The revised English book numbers Start Turn as an eighth phase. A second encounter still starts at Regroup.

Alien selection is explained only as a setup procedure. For the chosen alien, read its role, timing, mandatory/optional instruction, and any special setup directly from your own component. Technology, expansion modules, and other variants are outside this guide.
